# Race Desk editorial association foundation

This foundation records curated relationships between a published article and a stable registry `raceId`. The three pilot records have source-specific review notes in [the evidence record](race-desk-r05-evidence.md); they are curation decisions, not a claim of human approval or verification of sporting claims. Editorial ownership clearance is still required before public integration.

`data/race-desk/editorial.v1.json` is an explicit allowlist. Each article/race pair has a reviewed publication day and owned article evidence: either a SHA-256 of the complete source file with body-line quotes, or a digest of extracted published headings and passages with passage quotes. An article may relate to multiple races only through separate records. `sessions: null` means no session association; a nonempty array may identify supported registry session kinds and optionally their matching provider keys, with evidence references for each. References confirm exact text, not its meaning: a reviewer must establish the event edition and session in context. No keyword, title, tag, date proximity, historical mention or entity identity automatically creates a relationship.

`selectEditorialAssociations` in `scripts/race-desk/editorial.mjs` is a pure, fail-closed selector. Its caller supplies the registry, article metadata, evidence bytes, IDs of independently checked public canonical pages, and a valid Athens publication day. It rejects unknown or duplicate pairs, unpublished/future articles, publication-day or evidence drift, unsupported sessions and invalid paths. Missing associations are valid. Any error invalidates the whole selection; callers must not publish a partial result. The returned links preserve the canonical article URL and existing title and date. It does not change articles, bylines, authoring data or the entity approval gates.

Run `node scripts/race-desk/editorial-report.mjs YYYY-MM-DD` from the repository after installing dependencies. It checks the current public pages and evidence and prints deterministic counts and IDs for every registered race; invalid inputs exit nonzero. The day is explicit so a historical report is reproducible. On 2026-11-01 the pilot covers Singapore 2026 (2) and Mexico 2026 (1); four other registered races have zero curated links. Zero is unknown coverage, not evidence of no relevant stories.

## Editorial boundaries reviewed

The E-01 research workspace and IndexedDB drafts are local and private; E-02 claim reviews are author-entered and stay with drafts, while E-03 preflight is advisory. E-04 archive discovery and E-05 comparisons return candidates and signals, not verified relationships. Those tools cannot grant publication or race-association approval. E-06 optional editorial review requires a separate human GitHub approval; E-07 corrections retain publication identity and use their own review and update-date rules. E-08 AI research is isolated from publication and its citations do not establish semantic support. Article source and publishing workflows retain their existing ownership and gates. Entity association approvals have their own subject and evidence semantics and cannot automatically be promoted to race editions. The blog index, related-article tag matching and publication metadata serve discovery and canonical presentation; none proves a particular race or session. Existing publication dates and canonical public pages are checked here, without editing those systems. No private notebook, claim ledger or draft is read by this foundation.

This is independent of R-04 results. R-03 provides the existing hub and R-01 provides stable identity; the R-05B public integration below repeats the publication and evidence checks on every build and presents empty coverage explicitly.

## Public GP Hub presentation (R-05B)

The "04 / ΙΣΤΟΡΙΕΣ" section of each GP Hub lists exactly the articles this selector returns for that `raceId`, newest first: the original title linked to its canonical article URL, and its publication date in Greek. Nothing else is shown: no summary, image, category, session label or confidence badge. A listed article is an editorial association with that Grand Prix, not a verification of every claim in it. A hub with no curated association says that no curated links have been recorded yet; it does not claim that no relevant article exists.

`readEditorialSelection` (`scripts/race-desk/editorial-files.mjs`) is the single file reader for the selector. It reads the manifest, evidence bytes and article metadata from the repository and checks the rendered public article page; packaged validation checks the page inside `dist`. The blog build passes its current posts and Athens day to `writeRaceDeskHubs`, after article pages are rendered. The writer computes the selection before preparing any page, so an invalid selection makes it write nothing and stops the build; it does not undo homepage sections the blog build already wrote. The standalone `node scripts/build/race-desk-hubs.mjs` and the validators use the committed `blog-source-cache.json`; run the blog build first when article metadata changes. With the same inputs, explicit day and build year, output is byte-identical. A future-dated associated article invalidates the whole selection rather than silently disappearing. Source and packaged validation require each hub's section to list exactly the selected links, titles and dates and to contain no association evidence. The manifest, source files, digests, reviewed revisions, notes and evidence paths are never serialized into visitor HTML or `dist`. Quotes are excerpts of public articles and may coincide with public text such as a hub title, so validation checks the private markers rather than quotes.

Public presentation of an association requires the editorial owner's explicit clearance, recorded on the pull request that introduces or changes it and tied to its reviewed revision. A curation record, a passing digest or a merged foundation is not that clearance.

## Associating a new article

1. An author or reviewer proposes an exact `articleId` and registry `raceId`.
2. A reviewer reads the published article and confirms it concerns that edition (season and Grand Prix), not just the circuit, a driver or a historical mention.
3. Record evidence: the SHA-256 of the complete `source.txt` with 1-based body-line quotes, or, for a source-less article, the `published-passages-v1` digest with passage quotes. Calculate the digest independently, never by copying a tool's output unchecked.
4. Record `publishedDay` from the article's Athens publication day and keep `sessions: null` unless a session is separately established with its own references.
5. Open a pull request that changes only `data/race-desk/editorial.v1.json` (and the evidence record), stating any ambiguity, and whether the article is a betting preview or opinion.
6. Run `node scripts/race-desk/editorial-report.mjs YYYY-MM-DD`, `npm run test:race-desk` and `npm run build:public`, and check the generated hub page.
7. The editorial owner approves public presentation on that pull request; only then is it merged and deployed.

Removing an association is the same reviewable manifest change. A later edit to an associated article's source changes its digest and stops the build until the record is re-reviewed. Candidate lists from search, entity hubs or AI tools are leads only.
