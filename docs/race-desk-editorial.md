# Race Desk editorial association foundation

This foundation records curated relationships between a published article and a stable registry `raceId`. It does not add links to public pages. The three pilot records have source-specific review notes in [the evidence record](race-desk-r05-evidence.md); they are curation decisions, not a claim of human approval or verification of sporting claims. Editorial ownership clearance is still required before public integration.

`data/race-desk/editorial.v1.json` is an explicit allowlist. Each article/race pair has a reviewed publication day and owned article evidence: either a SHA-256 of the complete source file with body-line quotes, or a digest of extracted published headings and passages with passage quotes. An article may relate to multiple races only through separate records. `sessions: null` means no session association; a nonempty array may identify supported registry session kinds and optionally their matching provider keys, with evidence references for each. References confirm exact text, not its meaning: a reviewer must establish the event edition and session in context. No keyword, title, tag, date proximity, historical mention or entity identity automatically creates a relationship.

`selectEditorialAssociations` in `scripts/race-desk/editorial.mjs` is a pure, fail-closed selector. Its caller supplies the registry, article metadata, evidence bytes, IDs of independently checked public canonical pages, and a valid Athens publication day. It rejects unknown or duplicate pairs, unpublished/future articles, publication-day or evidence drift, unsupported sessions and invalid paths. Missing associations are valid. Any error invalidates the whole selection; callers must not publish a partial result. The returned links preserve the canonical article URL and existing title and date. It does not change articles, bylines, authoring data or the entity approval gates.

Run `node scripts/race-desk/editorial-report.mjs YYYY-MM-DD` from the repository after installing dependencies. It checks the current public pages and evidence and prints deterministic counts and IDs for every registered race; invalid inputs exit nonzero. The day is explicit so a historical report is reproducible. On 2026-11-01 the pilot covers Singapore 2026 (2) and Mexico 2026 (1); four other registered races have zero curated links. Zero is unknown coverage, not evidence of no relevant stories.

## Editorial boundaries reviewed

The E-01 research workspace and IndexedDB drafts are local and private; E-02 claim reviews are author-entered and stay with drafts, while E-03 preflight is advisory. E-04 archive discovery and E-05 comparisons return candidates and signals, not verified relationships. Those tools cannot grant publication or race-association approval. E-06 optional editorial review requires a separate human GitHub approval; E-07 corrections retain publication identity and use their own review and update-date rules. E-08 AI research is isolated from publication and its citations do not establish semantic support. Article source and publishing workflows retain their existing ownership and gates. Entity association approvals have their own subject and evidence semantics and cannot automatically be promoted to race editions. The blog index, related-article tag matching and publication metadata serve discovery and canonical presentation; none proves a particular race or session. Existing publication dates and canonical public pages are checked here, without editing those systems. No private notebook, claim ledger or draft is read by this foundation.

This is independent of R-04 results. R-03 provides the existing hub and R-01 provides stable identity; a future integration must repeat publication and evidence checks, secure editorial clearance, and decide how to present empty coverage before exposing links.
